A man jumped out of a jet, at night, into a storm, with two hundred thousand dollars. He was never seen again.

November, 1971. A quiet man in a dark suit and a clip-on tie boards a flight in Portland.

He orders a bourbon. Then he hands the flight attendant a note.

She thinks he's hitting on her. He leans in. "Miss, you'd better look at that note. I have a bomb."

Red sticks. Wires. A battery.

He wants two hundred thousand dollars in twenties, and four parachutes.

The FBI pays. But first, they photograph every single bill.

In Seattle, he lets the passengers go, and sends the jet back into the night.

Over the forests of Washington, he lowers the rear stairs. And he's gone.

Into freezing rain. In loafers.

A reporter got his name wrong. The legend became D.B. Cooper.

The manhunt found nothing. No body. No parachute. For nine years.

Then a boy digging on a riverbank found rotting bundles of twenties. The serial numbers matched.

How they got there, no one knows. The rest of the money has never turned up.

In 2016, the FBI closed the case. The only unsolved hijacking in American history.

And all he left behind was that clip-on tie.
