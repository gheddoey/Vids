# EscobarRF — script v2 (screenwriting directive)

## 1. Concept
60 s vertical historical crime documentary, adult audience, tone: tense, tragic, unglamorous.
Intended effect: pulled in by a man running out of road; seduced by the rise; chilled by the cost; the
ending lands on the victims, not on him.

## 2. Premise & direction
Not "the life of a drug lord" but **a man who ran out of places to hide**. The story is the trap closing:
everything he builds (money, power, a prison of his own) becomes a hiding place, and each one fails.

## 3. Hook & ending
- **Hook:** cold open in medias res on the last day: date, place, a man who is out of places to hide.
  It sets the central question (how does the most hunted man in Colombia end up on a rooftop?).
- **Ending:** the funeral, turned against him: "Thousands came to his funeral. Far more had gone to
  theirs." The emotional payoff goes to his victims.
- **Setup → payoff:** the phone. He "kept calling his son" (beat 7) → "one of those calls gave him away"
  (beat 8). The rooftop: opened in the hook, closed in beat 8.

## 6. Self-critique (first draft → revisions)
- Draft hook "the most wanted man on Earth" → cliché and unverifiable → "most hunted man in Colombia".
- Draft beat 2 "Twenty years earlier this city was his" → inaccurate (1973 he was a petty criminal) → "used to be his".
- Draft ending "Colombia is still living with what he left behind" → abstract, echoes the guide's generic line →
  the funeral contrast puts the weight on the victims.
- Draft beat 8 included "Police stormed the house" → cut; the visual does it and the line was over length.
- "He bought his way into Congress" → editorialising, not provable → "He even won a seat".
- Weakness that remains: beat 3 is dense (four ideas in 7 s); it works only if the cuts are fast. Beat 7 has no
  dialogue; a one-word phone line from Pablo ("¿Juan Pablo?") was considered and left out to keep it narrated.
