# Global Visual Quality Directive: World-Building, Prop Design & B-Roll

(User's standing instruction, Nov 2026. Applies to EVERY video, together with the Six Rules,
the Production System and the Set Design Directive.)

Create visually rich, cohesive, cinematic environments. Every object, prop, sign, background element
and B-roll shot must feel deliberately designed and professionally executed. Never settle for generic,
low-effort assets.

## 1. Object design: no generic placeholders
Applies to road/street signs, storefronts, ads, posters, labels, billboards, furniture, architecture,
vehicles, machinery, weapons, tools, clothing, accessories, environmental and background props.
Never "basic shape + text = done". A STOP sign is not a flat red octagon with white text; it has:
- designed geometry and proportions
- an interesting frame and mounting structure (post, bracket, bolts)
- fitting materials and surface detail
- real thickness / dimensionality
- wear, scratches, fading where appropriate
- lighting and shadows consistent with the scene
- a design matching the art direction (blocky, 64-px painted textures)

## 2. Unique design language per project
- Objects match the animation style, not imported generic assets.
- Important props get distinctive silhouettes, materials, colours, details.
- Custom geometry + textures; no repetitive designs or obvious placeholders.
- Everything looks like one art department made it.
- Consider location, period, culture, genre, atmosphere. A documentary, a sci-fi piece, a comedy and a
  crime thriller must not share one object-design language.
- Use real references for construction, then adapt to our visual language.

## 3. Environmental storytelling
Lived-in, not assembled: layered architecture and background structures, functional furniture/objects,
signs/posters/decor, roads/sidewalks/vegetation/infrastructure, wear and imperfections, small details
that tell the place's history and purpose. Every detail adds atmosphere or story. No clutter for its
own sake; every object has a reason to exist.

## 4. Cinematic B-roll (not filler)
- **Establishing:** wides for geography + atmosphere, aerial/elevated views, slow reveals.
- **Environmental details:** close-ups of signs, documents, objects, architecture; rain, dust, smoke,
  reflections, moving vegetation; objects showing period/culture/setting.
- **Character B-roll:** hands on objects, silhouettes in the environment, walking/waiting/observing/acting,
  subtle expressions and body language.
- **Inserts:** maps, photos, newspaper layouts, documents, evidence; vehicles passing; slow pushes toward
  meaningful objects; foreground details for depth.

## 5. B-roll must have purpose
Each B-roll shot must do at least one of: establish a location, give historical/context info, support
the narration, create atmosphere, build suspense, reveal character/world details, create emotion,
connect two scenes. Let visuals carry information instead of relying only on the VO.

## 6. Camera variety
Mix: wide establishing, medium, close-up, extreme close-up, low angle, high angle, over-the-shoulder,
tracking, dolly, slow push-in / pull-out, composed static shots. Movement communicates emotion and
directs attention. Don't move constantly; static can be just as strong. Don't repeat one angle.

## 7. Depth and detail
Every shot: FG/MG/BG separation, atmospheric perspective, lighting + shadow, reflections and material
response, environmental movement, layered set dressing, composition + negative space, focus and visual
hierarchy. No flat empty sets, nothing that looks pasted in.

## 8. Reference research
With internet access, research references before building important assets/environments: real objects,
architecture and locations, historical photos, material/texture refs, lighting and cinematography refs,
animation/production techniques. Don't copy; use refs to make original assets in our art direction.

## 9. Quality control (ask before calling an asset/shot finished)
- Matches the visual style? Convincing geometry and proportions? Intentionally designed?
- Enough detail without clutter? Fits the environment? Lighting makes sense?
- Would it pass in a professional animation? Is there a better way to show this visually?
- Primitive placeholder with text slapped on → redesign. Generic/repetitive/disconnected B-roll → replace.

## Final standard
Treat every frame as a composition from a professionally produced animated film. Don't trade visual
quality for speed on assets/shots that deserve attention. The goal is a coherent, distinctive, richly
detailed cinematic world, not merely a populated scene.
